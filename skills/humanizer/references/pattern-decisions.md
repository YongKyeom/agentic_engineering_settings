# Pattern decisions

Consult only the section relevant to an actual editing problem. These examples illustrate wording changes, not permission to invent missing context. Preserve the full claim, conditions, uncertainty, attribution, and authorial voice throughout.

## Inflation and attribution

**Importance and promotion.** Replace empty praise with a direct statement already supported by the input. Words such as “crucial,” “robust,” or “혁신적” are not forbidden when they express a supported, relevant claim. Keep intentional promotional tone when the user requests it.

> Before: The update represents a major leap forward by adding offline mode.
>
> After: The update adds offline mode.

**Superficial analysis.** Remove a trailing “highlighting,” “showcasing,” or “~을 보여 줍니다” when it merely repeats an assertion. Keep a real causal or interpretive claim and its evidence.

> Before: 서버는 요청을 기록하여 요청 이력을 남깁니다.
>
> After: 서버는 요청 이력을 기록합니다.

**Vague authority and media lists.** Preserve attribution that limits whose view is being reported. Do not turn “experts believe” into an unattributed fact or supply a fabricated survey. If the source is unspecified, a wording edit can retain that limitation; verification is a separate task.

> Before: 일부 연구자는 이 방법이 유효할 가능성이 있을 수 있다고 봅니다.
>
> After: 일부 연구자는 이 방법이 유효할 수 있다고 봅니다.

A list of publications can be necessary evidence. Delete it only if the requested edit permits removing that information; never replace it with an invented interview or quotation.

**Formulaic challenges, future outlooks, and conclusions.** Cut repeated praise or generic optimism when it adds nothing. Keep actual limitations, plans, caveats, and the relationships between them. A required conclusion section can remain useful.

> Before: In conclusion, the company plans to open two stores next year, marking an exciting new chapter.
>
> After: The company plans to open two stores next year.

## Wording and rhythm

**Stock vocabulary and copula avoidance.** Prefer the shorter expression when it has the same meaning. “Serves as” can describe a real temporary role; “is” may erase that distinction. Preserve field-specific terms such as a statistical “robust” estimator.

> Before: The file serves as a temporary backup until migration finishes.
>
> Keep: The file serves as a temporary backup until migration finishes.

**Forced contrast and negative parallelism.** Simplify rhetorical “not just X, but Y” only when X carries no necessary content. Preserve corrections, exclusions, genuine comparison, and legal or scientific negation. A direct sentence can still contain “not.”

> Before: 이 설정은 단순히 알림을 끄는 것이 아니라, 알림을 완전히 끄는 기능입니다.
>
> After: 이 설정은 알림을 완전히 끕니다.

> Before: 이 결과는 인과관계가 아니라 상관관계를 보여 준다.
>
> Keep: 이 결과는 인과관계가 아니라 상관관계를 보여 준다.

**Groups of three, synonym cycling, and false ranges.** Retain every real list item. Repeat the same technical term instead of cycling through near-synonyms. Turn a false “from X to Y” range into a list only when it denotes topics rather than actual endpoints or an intentional figure of speech.

> Before: 이 문서는 설치, 문제 해결, 계정 설정의 세 가지 주제에 대한 내용을 다룹니다.
>
> After: 이 문서는 설치, 문제 해결, 계정 설정을 다룹니다.

**Filler and uncertainty.** Cut duplicate modality, not the remaining uncertainty or its scope. “May,” “under these conditions,” “as of,” and “not established” can carry essential information.

> Before: It could potentially possibly affect results in small samples.
>
> After: It may affect results in small samples.

**Personality and rhythm.** Vary structure where mechanical repetition impedes reading. Preserve deliberate repetition, neutral reporting, dialect, register, and an author's restraint. Do not manufacture “I feel,” humor, tangents, or emotional detail to humanize a formal report.

## Formatting and chatbot artifacts

**Em dashes, boldface, inline-header lists, headings, emojis, and quotes.** Change formatting when it distracts, repeats, or conflicts with the destination. These devices are not inherently artificial. Keep useful lists, intentional emoji, publication-required title case, smart quotes, and exact quotations. Formatting changes must preserve content and relationships.

**Chatbot correspondence.** In a standalone article, remove accidental “I hope this helps” or “Would you like me to…” scaffolding. In an email or conversation, a greeting, subject line, or genuine request may belong to the author's message.

**Knowledge limits and disclaimers.** Remove a model's irrelevant training-cutoff boilerplate if it was accidentally pasted into an artifact. Retain an actual evidence limit, reporting date, jurisdiction, safety condition, or uncertainty. Do not replace “the founding date is unknown” with a guessed date.

**Flattery and generic positivity.** Remove reflexive “great question” or unsupported optimistic conclusions when they do not serve the intended relationship or genre. Preserve warranted thanks, praise, and an author's chosen warmth.
